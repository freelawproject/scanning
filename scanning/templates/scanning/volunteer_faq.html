Card review: questions and answers

Try real cards Back to my cards

Each card shows a small cut of a scanned page and the words three readers made of it. Your answer is the reading that matches the page. Nothing you answer changes the text at once: the answers are checked and written in later, and Undo takes one back until then.

The cards

What is a card?
One place on a page where the three readers did not agree. The card shows the cut of the page and each reading. Pick the one that matches the print, or type the right words.

Which part of the block is the card asking about?
The words around the place are greyed; the word in question is the underlined one. Answer for that word, and leave the greyed words as they are.
Which keys do what?
↓ and ↑ move between cards; the card in the middle of the screen is the selected one. → and ← move down and up its options, and Return chooses the outlined one. 1, 2 and 3 choose a reading, 0 leaves the place blank, e types the words, s skips (nothing is saved), u undoes, f opens the card to the whole page.
What does "Problem" mean?
The block is not text at all (bleed-through from the page behind, a stamp), the page is a bad scan that needs scanning again, or a black box on the page is in the wrong place.

Common patterns

One reading runs on past the block

A reader often continues into the next paragraph, the next column or the next page, and may even make words up. The three readings of one block can look like this:

  • Dorothy traveled on the yellow br-
  • Dorothy traveled on the yellow brick road
  • Dorothy traveled on the yellow brick road to Oz and …

We want what is plainly in the block on the page, even when the sentence goes on somewhere else. If the block ends at "br-", the first reading is the right one.

The ⊥ mark (an upside-down T), usually with a number: ⊥123

These are West's page markers, and we do not want them in the text for this round. Leave the mark and its number out: pick the reading without it, or type the words without it.

It can also land in the middle of a word, as in "perp⊥indicular123". There too the "⊥123" comes out and the word is "perpendicular".

Footnote numbers (superscripts)
Keep the number, as a plain number where it stands, the way the line reads. The superscript is rebuilt later from the numbers we find.
Runs of dots
An ellipsis or a row of dots that is on the page belongs in the text. Keep it as the page shows it.
